> Three candles where the middle one moved so fast it left a gap. Price often comes back to it.

## What it is
A fair value gap (FVG) is a three-candle pattern: the middle candle moves so strongly that the first candle's high and the third candle's low do not overlap (or, going down, the first candle's low and the third's high). That gap is price the market skipped — an imbalance. A bullish gap is also called BISI, a bearish one SIBI. Its midpoint is the consequent encroachment (CE). A gap lives through stages: open, tapped when price trades back into it, CE when price reaches its middle, filled when price trades through it. A bigger gap with nothing traded inside is a liquidity void.

## How to spot it
- Find a strong candle; look at the candle before it and the candle after it.
- Bullish: the first candle's high is below the third candle's low. Bearish: the first candle's low is above the third's high.
- The space between them is the gap; its middle is the CE.
- Bigger gaps from real displacement matter more than tiny ones.

## How it is traded
The FVG is the core entry. In the direction of your bias, wait for price to retrace into the gap and enter there — at its edge or its CE. The stop goes beyond the swing that started the move (beyond the low or high that made the sweep, if there was one). Target the next liquidity.
