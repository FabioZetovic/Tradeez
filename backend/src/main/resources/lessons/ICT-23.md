> A fair value gap that failed: price closed straight through it, and now it works the other way.

## What it is
An inversion fair value gap (IFVG) is a gap that price closed through — beyond its far edge. The gap did not hold, and ICT reads it as flipped: a bullish gap that price closed below becomes resistance, a bearish gap that price closed above becomes support. It is one of the clearest signs that the other side has taken control.

## How to spot it
- Find a fair value gap.
- Watch for a candle that closes beyond its far edge — a close, not just a wick.
- From then on the gap is read in the opposite direction.

## How it is traded
After a bullish gap is inverted, look to sell when price comes back up into it; after a bearish gap is inverted, look to buy when price comes back down into it. The stop goes beyond the gap's far side or the high or low that made the move; the target is the next liquidity in the new direction.
