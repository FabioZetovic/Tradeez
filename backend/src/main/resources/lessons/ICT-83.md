> How much to risk, and why a losing streak hurts more than it looks.

## What it is
Risk is measured in R: one R is what you lose if the stop is hit. Position size follows from it: the dollars you are willing to lose divided by what one contract loses at your stop. On NQ a tick is $5.00 a contract; on MNQ $0.50. Losses compound: losing 2% ten times in a row leaves you about 18% down, and getting back from a drawdown always takes a bigger gain than the loss — 50% down needs 100% up.

## How to spot it
- Decide the risk per trade first — a fixed share of the account.
- Contracts = risk in dollars ÷ (stop in ticks × tick value), always rounded down.
- Know your daily and overall drawdown limits before the session — prop firm evaluations live on them.

## How it is traded
Size every trade from its stop, never from a feeling. Keep the risk per trade small enough that a normal losing streak does not break your account or your evaluation, and stop trading for the day when the daily limit is reached.
