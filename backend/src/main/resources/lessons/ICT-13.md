> Price runs the stops beyond a level: does it snap back (a sweep) or keep going (a run)?

## What it is
When price trades beyond a swing high or low it takes the liquidity resting there. If the candle closes back inside — beyond for a moment, then rejected — it is a sweep: the stops were the target, and the move may reverse. If price closes beyond and keeps going, it is a run: the level was broken for real. A sweep traded as a reversal is what ICT calls a turtle soup.

## How to spot it
- Price trades a tick or more beyond a swing high or low.
- Sweep: the same candle (or the next few) closes back inside the old range.
- Run: candles close beyond the level and hold there.

## How it is traded
After a sweep, look for the reversal: a market structure shift the other way, then an entry in the gap it leaves. Stops are placed on recent highs and lows after a liquidity sweep — just beyond the high or low that made the sweep. After a run, do not fade it: trade with the break instead.
