> Where the stop goes, and where the target goes.

## What it is
Stops are placed on recent highs and lows after a liquidity sweep: just beyond the high or low that made the sweep. That is the price that proves the idea wrong — if the market goes back there, the sweep failed. A stop just behind your entry sits inside normal noise and gets taken for no reason. Targets are the liquidity on the other side: the next resting highs or lows, taken in parts where it makes sense.

## How to spot it
- Find the high or low that took the liquidity before your entry.
- Put the stop a tick or two beyond it — not at the old swing it swept, which price has already traded through.
- Put the target at the next resting liquidity in the trade's direction.

## How it is traded
Stop beyond the sweep's extreme, target at the opposing liquidity, and check the reward is worth the risk before you enter. Take partial profit at the first liquidity if the next one is far.
