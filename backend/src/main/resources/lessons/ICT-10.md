> Where the stops are: above old highs and below old lows. Price is drawn to them.

## What it is
Buy-side liquidity (BSL) is the cluster of buy stops resting above a swing high — shorts protecting themselves and breakout traders waiting to buy. Sell-side liquidity (SSL) is the sell stops below a swing low. The most obvious ones are the reference highs and lows everyone can see: the previous day's high and low (PDH, PDL), the previous week's, and the session highs and lows. ICT's idea is that price moves from one pool of liquidity to the next.

## How to spot it
- Above every swing high that has not been traded through rests buy-side liquidity.
- Below every swing low that has not been traded through rests sell-side liquidity.
- The more obvious the level — the previous day's high or low, a session extreme, equal highs — the more stops are there.
- Once a candle trades beyond the level, that liquidity has been taken.

## How it is traded
Liquidity gives you targets and warnings. Target the nearest untaken pool in the direction of your trade, and be careful entering right in front of one against you: price likes to run it first. Never put your own stop exactly where everyone else's is.
