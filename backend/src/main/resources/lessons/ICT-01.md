> The turning points of the chart: every other idea in ICT is built on them.

## What it is
A swing high is a candle whose high is higher than the candles on both sides of it; a swing low is a candle whose low is lower than both neighbours. They are the places price turned. Not every swing matters equally: a swing high with lower swing highs on both sides of it is an intermediate-term high, and the same again one level up is a long-term high — those are the ones traders watch.

## How to spot it
- Look for a candle that sticks out above (or below) the candles right next to it.
- It is only confirmed when the candle after it closes — until then it can still be beaten.
- Compare it with the swings around it: a high that stands above its neighbouring swing highs is a stronger, intermediate-term high.

## How it is traded
Swings are not traded on their own; they are where everything else is measured from. Stops rest just beyond them, so they are where liquidity sits. A break of a swing tells you structure has changed, and a swing that gets swept and rejected is often the start of a move the other way.
