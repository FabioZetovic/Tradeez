> Accumulation, manipulation, distribution: how ICT reads a day.

## What it is
Power of three describes a day in three phases: accumulation near the open, a manipulation move against the day's real direction (the Judas swing — a false move that takes liquidity), then distribution in the real direction. A bullish day opens, drops below the open, then rallies to close above it; a bearish day mirrors it.

## How to spot it
- Mark the day's midnight open.
- Bullish: the low comes first, below the open, then the high; the day closes above the open.
- Bearish: the high comes first, above the open, then the low; the day closes below the open.

## How it is traded
In a bullish bias, wait for the manipulation down below the open — the Judas swing — and look for longs after it; in a bearish bias, wait for the false move up. The Judas swing at the London or New York open is the classic session-open reversal.
